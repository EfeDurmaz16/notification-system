# Architecture decisions and candidate contributions

The candidate made the product choices below. The coding assistant proposed alternatives, implemented the selected behavior and ran the experiments. A measured result is attributed to its test. A design idea is attributed to the person who proposed or selected it. The two largest latency changes came from separate decisions and separate workloads.

## From design discussion to observable behavior

## A standalone request arriving during a campaign

The candidate identified a missing product distinction: even at the same priority, a standalone notification may be time-sensitive. The candidate chose a standalone lane inside each priority, asked to preserve progress for batches under continuous standalone traffic and ruled out borrowing the normal-priority topic. The assistant compared 10%, 20% and 40% batch weights before choosing the delegated 20% default. The weight applies when both lanes have work visible to the scheduler. An idle lane's capacity is available to the other. [Contention experiments and default](../reports/ingress-share/README.md)

An earlier prototype borrowed the idle normal-priority topic. Compared with the old shared route, standalone latency fell from 109.760 s to 0.583 s and from 116.117 s to 0.163 s in two trials. The candidate kept the independent read path and requested dedicated lanes at the original priority. This preserved the priority contract when normal traffic returned. [Prototype comparison and limitations](https://github.com/EfeDurmaz16/notification-system/blob/c605af9/reports/ingress-probe/README.md)

| Matched comparison during a 10,000-SMS campaign | Time from creation to recorded provider acceptance |
| --- | ---: |
| One-item batch, with 9,492 campaign items outstanding at creation | 108.312 s |
| Standalone with the same recipient, priority and content, created 7.19 ms later | 100.46 ms |
| All eight standalone probes in that run | 100.46 to 595.51 ms |

The first matched pair differed by about **1,078×**. Both probes ran on the same scheduler. The ratio describes the latency these two paths experienced under this backlog. It measures neither a version-to-version throughput gain nor a fleet-wide p95 improvement. [Per-ID records](../reports/ingress-share/campaign-10000.json)

## A push batch waiting behind an SMS campaign

A separate experiment located head-of-line blocking before the worker could schedule a push. Separating work after the shared fetch had little effect. The candidate agreed to test independent broker readers while retaining the shared worker pool, then challenged keeping the old reader solely because the measured SMS cost slightly exceeded the original budget. [Post-fetch experiment](../reports/channel-isolation/README.md) · [Independent-reader experiment](../reports/channel-readers/README.md)

| Matched trials | Loaded batch push p95, shared to independent readers | SMS POSTs/s during probes, shared to independent readers | Original maximum SMS loss |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| A1/B1 | 42.978 s to 187 ms | 83.114 to 78.416 (5.65% loss) | 5%, failed |
| A2/B2 | 45.736 s to 246 ms | 82.717 to 77.541 (6.26% loss) | 5%, failed |

**Release decision.** The initial rule required batch push p95 at or below two seconds and SMS loss at or below 5% in **each** matched pair. After seeing the results, the candidate accepted a revised **7%** SMS loss budget for this workload and selected per-channel batch readers. Both pairs qualify under the revised budget. The original [gate results](../reports/channel-readers/gate.json) retain the outcomes under the predeclared rule. The cost is measured during the push probes. Two local pairs do not establish performance for every campaign.

Both reader variants kept the same worker pool, channel limits and cancellation guarantee. Across 16,416 IDs, 16,384 accepted IDs had one POST each and all 32 cancelled IDs had zero. Migration also had to preserve work queued in the earlier topic format. The cutover and rollback test verified 15 accepted IDs with one POST each and one cancelled ID with zero. [Measured workload and results](../reports/channel-readers/README.md) · [Cutover and rollback](../reports/channel-cutover/README.md)

## What can a replacement worker learn after a lost acknowledgement?

On 21 November 2026 the candidate used a bank ledger analogy to ask whose record could settle an `unknown` notification. They proposed looking up the stable `X-Notification-ID` in the provider's history and asked what an attempt timestamp or a log immediately before the POST could establish. A recorded send claim can survive either a crash before the POST or a crash after provider acceptance. A timestamp helps correlate attempts. Both crash sequences can leave the same local record. In the [worker-kill experiment](../reports/channel-cutover/rebalance/README.md), the fixture saw all six unresolved IDs before the worker died.

| Evidence available after the crash | What it supports |
| --- | --- |
| Webhook.site captured a POST with the matching ID | An operator can confirm arrival at the endpoint and keep the outcome `unknown`. An endpoint can capture a request and return a rejection. An empty search also leaves the outcome unresolved. [Request history and header search](https://docs.webhook.site/api/requests.html#get-requests) |
| A provider guarantees idempotency for a documented period | The existing worker can retry the same notification ID and parameters within the remaining key lifetime and attempt budget. A valid acknowledgement confirms acceptance even if the worker cannot determine which attempt first succeeded. [Delivery policy](decisions.md) |
| A provider can look up our ID or send an authenticated callback correlated with it | A future integration could record the provider's authoritative acceptance receipt and reconcile the notification to `accepted`. Confirmation that the recipient received it would require a separate delivery receipt. |

**Decision for v1.1.0.** Keep Webhook.site capture history as evidence for manual investigation. The worker records acceptance from a valid HTTP 202 acknowledgement with a message ID, accepted status and timestamp. **Open for a real provider:** verify which ID can be queried when the first response is lost, what each returned status guarantees, and how long query records and idempotency keys remain available. Those answers determine whether to automate reconciliation. [Current provider contract](webhook-site.md) · [Acknowledgement handling](../internal/delivery/engine.go)
